Andrea [신영, 新泳, shinyoung] is a research scientist based in Los Angeles exploring autofiction, hybrid media arts, and the digital humanities.

With current attention on local-first, decentralized networking tools, my aim is to re-imagine educational systems and collaborative work based on responsible, enriching, digital practices. My framework for arts-based research draws from what Mimi Khúc names as a "pedagogy of unwellness," an attunement toward the multivarious forms of harm affecting students, artists, researchers, and cultural workers within and beyond the university. I apply reparative approaches to the social sciences, taking up Eve Tuck's call for "desire-based research" grounded in complexity, hope, and the deep intersectionalities necessary for any knowledge to be meaningful. I find inspiration in scholars, writers, and practitioners Sora Han, Avgi Saketopoulou, Eirliani Abdul Rahman, Hortense Spillers, Saidiya Hartman amongst many others traversing unexplored terrain in their urgent offerings of truth.

In the past, I have written on reproductive autonomy, digital bodies, and XR; my current work models decentralized knowledge systems and co-creation protocols for the social sciences and creative research. I am an alumni of the MIT Comparative Media Studies Program, MIT Open Documentary Lab and Co-Creation Studio, Duke Center for Documentary Studies, and Duke Literature Program, and has served on selection committees for the Fulbright Association (Visiting Scholars Program) and Peabody Awards (Interactive Media Section). I have also worked previously with CultureHub Los Angeles, MIT Transmedia Storytelling Initiative, Universidad Nacional Autónoma de México, Los Angeles Contemporary Exhibitions, U.S. Embassy in Rabat, and the Seoul Institute of the Arts.

I am currently in the fifth-year of my PhD in Media Arts + Practice at the University of Southern California.
